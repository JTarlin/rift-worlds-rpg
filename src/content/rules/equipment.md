## What you carry

Equipment is described by what it lets you do in the fiction. Ordinary gear does not add numerical bonuses. It grants permission, improves effect, or prevents peril when it is the right tool for the job.

## Load

| Load | Slots | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Light** | 5 | Unencumbered and inconspicuous |
| **Ready** | 8 | Prepared for danger |
| **Heavy** | 11 | Equipped for a siege, visibly burdened |

Most items occupy one slot. Bulky items occupy two. Tiny or incidental possessions are free unless scarcity makes them important.

## Weapons

| Weapon | Range | Traits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blade | Engaged | precise, concealable |
| Heavy weapon | Engaged | forceful, two-handed |
| Bow | Near/Far | quiet, two-handed |
| Firearm | Near/Far | loud, piercing |
| Rift implement | Varies | strange, volatile |

Traits shape position and effect. A piercing weapon may defeat armor; a loud weapon always announces itself.

## Armor

Light armor occupies one slot and can reduce one minor harm per scene. Heavy armor occupies three slots and can reduce one severe harm per expedition, but causes peril when silence, swimming, or speed matters.

## Useful gear

- Climbing kit
- Field medicine
- Lantern and fuel
- Survey instruments
- Repair tools
- Portable shelter
- Signal flares
- Trade goods

## Relics

Relics are impossible objects changed by the rifts. Each has a **gift** that breaks an ordinary limit, a **tell** that makes its use recognizable, and a **price** that appears when pushed too far.

Relics should create decisions, not replace them. Their power is dependable; their consequences are not.
